Privacy Policy

FraudPilot: High-risk order review for Shopify

Effective date: October 18, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Alpenglow Software LLC ("we," "us," "our"), operator of FraudPilot ("the App"), collects, uses, stores, and protects information when you install and use the App through the Shopify platform. FraudPilot is a human-review queue for orders that Shopify's risk engine flags as medium or high risk. It enriches each flagged order with the facts you need to make a call, lets you record your decision (approve, cancel, or tag), and keeps an immutable, append-only decision log you can export as chargeback-representment evidence. FraudPilot does not decide for you. The only change it makes to an order in Shopify is adding a review tag when a member of your staff clicks Tag. You can also look up any recent order by its number to see Shopify's risk details for it. See Section 1 for details.

1. Information We Collect

Data from Shopify APIs

The App requests the following Shopify access scopes:

  • read_orders: used to build and enrich the review queue. For each order Shopify flags as medium or high risk, the App reads and stores the order name/number, order date, order total and currency, line-item count, the line items themselves (product title, quantity, and line total), the payment gateway name(s), AVS (address verification) and CVV result codes, fulfillment status, and Shopify's own risk assessment and risk signals. For flagged orders placed by a customer account, the App also counts how many orders that customer placed before the flagged order, in total and within the 24 hours and 7 days before it (counts only; the other orders' contents are not read or stored). The App reads the network (IP) address recorded on the order, but stores it only in coarsened form (see "Protected Customer Data" below). The App does not access or store customer names, postal addresses, email addresses, phone numbers, or payment card information through this scope. To monitor that the App is working for your store, once a day we also read how many orders were created in the last day and in the last week, how many of the last week's orders Shopify flagged, and the order identifiers of up to 250 orders Shopify flagged since you installed the App (looking back at most 7 days). We compare those identifiers with your review queue to find any flagged order that did not arrive, and then discard them; they are not stored. The resulting counts are not stored in our database. They are included in a daily internal report sent only to us (see "Service monitoring" in Section 2 and the Resend entry in Section 5) and are used only for service monitoring.
  • Order Lookup (read_orders): when a member of your staff enters an order number on the Order Lookup page, the App reads that order's Shopify risk level and risk details, even if Shopify did not flag the order, and, on the Standard plan, the card's AVS and CVV result codes and the payment gateway name(s). Only orders from the last 60 days can be looked up. Shopify's risk details can include the approximate location of the IP address used to place the order and whether the billing or shipping address is consistent with it; the App displays these as Shopify provides them. The results are shown to you and are not stored: the App keeps no order number, order identifier, or risk details from a lookup. The order number you enter appears in the page address in your browser.
  • Card-testing alert (read_orders): to warn you about a possible card-testing run, every 15 minutes the App reads the abandoned checkouts created on your store in the last 24 hours. For each one it reads only the time it was created, its total and currency, and the title of its first line item. It does not read the name, email address, phone number, postal address, or IP address entered at checkout, any payment or card information, notes, or the checkout recovery link. These details are used to count checkouts and are then discarded; nothing about an individual checkout is stored. If the count for one product, or for your store as a whole, rises far above its usual level (or is very high and there is too little history to compare it with), the App stores an alert (see "Data we generate and store") and shows it to you on the App's home page.
  • Protection Checkup (no scope beyond those above): when you open the Protection Checkup page, the App reads your store's Shopify plan name, whether the store is a Shopify Plus or partner development store, and its customer accounts setting (whether customer accounts are off, optional, or required, and which version of customer accounts you use) and shows them to you on that page. To suggest how your payment capture setting is configured, it also reads your 25 most recent orders over read_orders, whether or not Shopify flagged them, and for each one only its sales channel and the type, status, and time of up to 10 payment transactions, including whether each was a card payment. It does not read card details or any customer details for this, and it uses only online-store card orders. If your store has classic customer accounts turned on, the App also reads your store's public login page (/account/login), as any visitor would, to see whether Shopify's hCaptcha is switched on for login and sign-up. The login page contains no customer data, and only the result (on, off, password-protected, or couldn't tell) is used. None of this is stored in our database: the login page result is kept in server memory for up to 2 minutes so the page does not re-read it on every click, and everything else is discarded once the page is shown. The App never changes any Shopify setting.
  • read_customers: used to read, for a flagged order, the Shopify Customer identifier (the customer's GID) and a short order-history summary for that customer: how many orders they placed before the flagged order, the date their customer account was created, and their lifetime amount spent. The identifier lets the App recognize a repeat buyer across your own prior decisions; the summary is shown to you as risk context (for example, a brand-new account placing its first order). These are the only customer fields the App reads. It does not read or store the customer's name, email address, phone number, or address, and it does not modify customer records.
  • write_orders: used only to add the tag fraudpilot-review to a flagged order when a member of your staff records a Tag decision in the App (available on the Standard plan). The App does not use this scope to read any additional data, and it does not use it to cancel, edit, refund, fulfill, or hold orders. If your store has not yet approved this permission, a Tag decision is recorded in FraudPilot only and the order is not tagged in Shopify.

The only change the App makes in Shopify is adding the fraudpilot-review tag to an order, and only when a member of your staff clicks Tag. It does not cancel, edit, refund, hold, or fulfill orders, and it does not change any customer record. Recording an Approve or Cancel decision in FraudPilot does not change the order in Shopify; you complete those actions in the Shopify Admin.

Protected Customer Data (Level 1)

The following fields are treated as Shopify Protected Customer Data at Level 1 (no name, address, email, or phone):

  • Coarsened network (IP) prefix and country: the App reads the client IP recorded on the order and immediately reduces it to a /24 network prefix (the final part of the address is discarded) and derives a country using a local geographic-IP database bundled with the App. The raw IP address is never stored, and no external lookup is performed for this. The coarsened prefix and country are used only to surface order velocity and geographic context for the flagged order, and to recognize whether the same network has appeared in your prior decisions.
  • Shopify Customer identifier (GID): used only to recognize repeat buyers across your own past decisions, as described above.
  • Customer order-history summary: the number of orders the customer placed before the flagged order (in total, and within the prior 24 hours and 7 days), their account creation date, and their lifetime amount spent. Used only to give you risk context on that flagged order; stored with the order's review artifacts and decision-log snapshot, and deleted with them (see Section 6).

The App does not request or access any Level 2 Protected Customer Data (name, address, email, or phone). Those fields are withheld by Shopify unless an app is separately approved for them, and FraudPilot does not request that approval.

Data collected through the Shopify session

Shopify provides the following data as part of the standard app authentication process:

  • Shop domain
  • Staff member name, email address, and user ID
  • Account role (store owner or collaborator) and email verification status
  • Locale preference
  • Session tokens, access tokens, and refresh tokens

This session data is managed by Shopify's official session storage library and is required for the App to function within the Shopify Admin. The App additionally stores the staff user identifier derived from the session with each recorded decision, so the decision log accurately attributes who made each call.

Data we generate and store

As you use the App, we store:

  • Review queue items: the flagged orders awaiting your review, and their queue state (for example, needs decision, snoozed, decided).
  • Enrichment snapshot: the order facts and risk signals described above, captured at review time so the record reflects what was known when the decision was made.
  • Decision log entries (append-only): for each decision, the order identifier, the acting staff user identifier, the action taken (approve, cancel, or tag), your reason or note, a snapshot of the risk facts at decision time (including the customer order-history summary above), the associated Shopify Customer identifier, and the coarsened /24 network prefix.
  • Dispute outcomes: if you record how a chargeback or dispute resolved (for example, "won in representment"), we store that outcome against the related decision.
  • Card-testing alerts: when the App detects an unusual burst of abandoned checkouts, it stores the product title the burst concentrates on (if any), the number of abandoned checkouts in the hour, the usual hourly number for that product or for your store (when known), the typical checkout total and its currency, the times the hour started and ended, when the burst was detected, and when you dismissed the alert. An alert contains no customer, checkout, or order identifiers.
  • Protection Checkup answers: for each item on the Protection Checkup checklist, the answer you chose (done, not yet, or doesn't apply), that you set it, and when it last changed. The answers contain no customer, checkout, or order data.
  • Store contact email: your store's contact email address, read from Shopify and stored so we can send the daily review digest (when enabled). This is your own merchant email, not customer data.
  • Usage and setup records: for your store (not per staff member), how many times the App's home page was opened (counted at most once per hour) and when it was last opened, when an order's review page was first opened, the status of the one-time import of recently flagged orders, when the setup guide was shown, clicked, completed, or dismissed, and each time the Order Lookup page found an order, together with the Shopify risk level (high, medium, low, or none) of that order. The order number and order identifier are not recorded. We also record whether and when the App asked Shopify to show its App Store review prompt, how many times it has asked, the response code Shopify returned, and when the App may ask again (whether you left a review is not recorded). For stores on, or moving to or from, the paid FraudPilot plan, we record each live check of the subscription plan: how long it took, the plan before and after it, and how many page loads reused the previous check. See "Service monitoring" in Section 2.
  • Public product count: the number of published products, read once a day from your storefront's public meta.json page (no access scope is used). It is not stored in our database; it appears only in the daily internal report described in Section 2.

Data we do NOT collect

  • Customer or shopper names, postal addresses, email addresses, or phone numbers
  • Raw (full) IP addresses
  • Payment card or banking information
  • Product catalog, inventory, or theme code (the App reads two public storefront pages, described above, only to count published products and to check whether hCaptcha is on, and keeps nothing else from them)
  • Storefront visitor or web-analytics behavior data
  • Marketing or advertising data

We do not use artificial intelligence or machine learning to process your data, and your data is not used to train AI or machine-learning models. If you choose to use Shopify Sidekick (see "Shopify Sidekick" in Section 2), the answers FraudPilot returns to it are processed by Shopify's AI assistant under Shopify's terms, not by us.

2. How We Use Your Information

We use the data we collect solely to provide and improve the App's services:

  • Review queue: we surface orders Shopify has flagged as medium or high risk so you can review them in one place rather than scanning the raw Orders list.
  • Enrichment: we assemble the order facts, risk signals, geographic and network context, order velocity, and your own prior decisions for the same buyer or network into a single review panel.
  • Decision recording: we record the decision you make (approve, cancel, or tag), together with your reason and the evidence snapshot, into the immutable decision log. For a Tag decision, we first add the fraudpilot-review tag to the order in Shopify and record the decision only after Shopify confirms it, and the log notes whether the tag was added in Shopify or recorded in FraudPilot only.
  • Card-testing alert: we count recent abandoned checkouts and warn you on the App's home page when there is an unusual burst, which can be a sign that stolen cards are being tested on your store. The alert is information for you; the App takes no action on any checkout, order, or customer.
  • Protection Checkup: we list Shopify settings that can make card testing harder, show you the two we can read, and remember the answers you mark so you can see what is left. We also show two hints that are never saved: a guess at your payment capture setting from how your recent online card orders were charged (read over read_orders), and, for classic customer accounts, the login page check described in Section 1. The App never changes your Shopify settings.
  • Shopify Sidekick (optional): if you ask Shopify Sidekick, Shopify's AI assistant in your Shopify Admin, about your review queue or recent decisions, Sidekick asks FraudPilot for a summary and FraudPilot returns it. On every plan, the summary contains how many orders are waiting (in total, high risk, and medium risk), how many are snoozed, how long the oldest has waited, and up to five of the most urgent waiting orders, each with its order name (for example, #1042, or its Shopify order ID if it has no name), its risk level, and how long it has waited. On the Standard plan, it also contains how many waiting orders show each of FraudPilot's own reason labels (for example, "CVV did not match" or "Brand-new customer account placing its first order"), how many orders those reasons were counted over and whether that covers only the most recent 200, and, for the last 30 days, how many orders were decided, your approve, cancel, and tag counts, and your approve rate. It contains no customer names, contact details, or customer identifiers, no network (IP) or location data, no order totals or line items, and no text from Shopify's risk assessment. FraudPilot does not receive your questions or Sidekick's replies, and it does not store the summaries it returns; a request can at most trigger the routine subscription plan check described under "Usage and setup records" in Section 1. Sidekick is operated by Shopify, which processes your conversations, including these summaries, under its own terms and privacy policy.
  • Representment evidence: we let you export the decision log and its evidence snapshots as a CSV file you can use to contest chargebacks.
  • Outcome tracking: we store dispute outcomes you record so you can see how your decisions held up.
  • Subscription management: we store your current billing plan to gate features appropriately.
  • App functionality: we use your shop domain and session data to authenticate requests and deliver the App within the Shopify Admin.
  • Service monitoring: we check that every order Shopify flags reaches your review queue, using the aggregate order counts described in Section 1 and your store's public product count, and we keep basic records of your use of the App (when it was last opened, how many times, which setup steps were completed, how often Order Lookup was used, and how many card-testing alerts were raised) so we can spot and fix problems.

Service-monitoring data is reviewed only by us, in a daily internal report that identifies your store only by an internal ID, not by its name or domain. We do not use it for sales outreach, pricing, benchmarking, or any purpose other than keeping the App working and improving its setup experience.

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for advertising, marketing, cross-store or advertising profiling, or automated decision-making. The App shows you risk context about a flagged order (such as the customer's order history with your store); it does not build profiles of customers across stores. FraudPilot makes no automated decision about any order or customer; every decision recorded in the App is one a member of your staff made.

For the purposes of applicable data protection laws, you (the merchant) are the data controller for the order and customer data made available through the App. Alpenglow Software LLC acts as a data processor, processing that data solely on your behalf and as described in this policy. For any data we collect directly from you (such as support communications) and for the service-monitoring records described in Section 2, we act as an independent data controller.

3. How We Process Order Data

Order and risk data flows through the App as follows:

  1. When Shopify flags an order as medium or high risk (or when the App reads a flagged order via the Admin API), the App fetches the order facts and risk signals over the read_orders scope, and the customer identifier and customer order-history summary over the read_customers scope.
  2. When you use Order Lookup, the App fetches the risk level and risk details of the order you entered, and on the Standard plan its AVS and CVV result codes and payment gateway name(s), over the read_orders scope. It does not fetch the customer identifier or the client IP for a lookup, and it does not store the result.
  3. Every 15 minutes, the App reads the created time, total, and first product title of your store's abandoned checkouts from the last 24 hours over the read_orders scope, counts them, and discards them. It stores a card-testing alert only when the count is far above the usual level for that product or for your store, or is very high with too little history to compare it with.
  4. When you open the Protection Checkup page, the App reads the sales channel and the payment transaction types, statuses, and times of your 25 most recent orders over the read_orders scope, uses them to suggest how your payment capture setting is configured, and discards them.
  5. The client IP on the order is reduced to a /24 network prefix and resolved to a country using a local geographic-IP database in the same step. The raw IP is discarded and never written to storage.
  6. The App assembles an enrichment snapshot from these facts, adds order-velocity and prior-decision context drawn only from your own store's records, and places the order in your review queue.
  7. When you make a decision, the App writes an append-only decision-log entry capturing the action, your reason, and the evidence snapshot as it stood at that moment. For a Tag decision on a store that has approved write_orders, the App first adds the fraudpilot-review tag to the order in Shopify; if Shopify does not confirm the tag, nothing is recorded and you are shown an error.
  8. Enrichment and decision records are displayed to you in the App and can be exported for representment.

The decision log is append-only: the App exposes no path to edit or delete a logged decision through normal use. This is what makes the log usable as tamper-resistant evidence. The only paths that remove decision-log rows are the data-deletion obligations described in Section 6 (customer redaction, shop redaction, or a deletion request you make).

4. Data Storage and Security

  • All data is stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted on Railway (United States).
  • Shopify access tokens are encrypted at rest using AES-256-GCM encryption.
  • All connections use HTTPS/TLS encryption in transit.
  • We store the client IP only in coarsened /24 form; the raw IP is never persisted.
  • We do not sell your data. We do not share your data with third parties for marketing, advertising, or profiling purposes.
  • In the event of a data breach affecting your data, we will notify you and Shopify within 24 hours of discovery, in accordance with Shopify's Partner Program Agreement and applicable law.

6. Data Retention

  • While installed: review queue items, enrichment snapshots, decision-log entries, dispute outcomes, card-testing alerts, and Protection Checkup answers are retained for as long as the App is installed on your store. The decision log is retained deliberately, because it is your representment evidence. Usage and setup records (Section 1) are also retained while the App is installed and deleted with your shop data.
  • Operator reports and alerts: the daily internal operator report identifies your store only by an internal ID, which is deleted with your shop data, so a past report can no longer be linked to your store. An alert about a failed privacy request that names your store is deleted from our mailbox once the request is resolved. Our email provider (Resend) keeps a copy of sent email for 30 days.
  • Tags in Shopify: a fraudpilot-review tag the App adds to an order is part of your Shopify order record, not data we hold. It stays on the order after you uninstall the App or after a deletion request. You can remove it in the Shopify Admin.
  • On uninstall: when you uninstall the App, Shopify sends a shop-redact request (typically 48 hours after uninstall). On receiving it, all data associated with your shop is permanently deleted, including sessions, queue items, enrichment snapshots, decision-log entries, dispute outcomes, card-testing alerts, Protection Checkup answers, and your shop record. Deletion is completed within the timeframe below.
  • GDPR / privacy shop-redact: if Shopify sends a shop data erasure request, all shop data is deleted within 30 days (and in practice promptly on receipt).
  • Customer redaction: on a customer redaction request (see Section 7), the review artifacts, decision-log rows, and dispute outcomes tied to the affected order(s) are deleted within 30 days.

7. Your Rights

You have the right to:

  • Access: view your review queue, enrichment snapshots, and full decision history in the App, and export the decision log as CSV.
  • Deletion: uninstalling the App deletes all your data (see Section 6). You may also contact us to request deletion without uninstalling.
  • Data portability: export your decision log from the App, or contact us to request an export of your stored data in a machine-readable format.
  • Correction: contact us if you believe any stored data is inaccurate. Note that the decision log is append-only by design; corrections are made by recording a new decision rather than altering a historical entry.

Regarding your customers. FraudPilot does not collect customer names, addresses, email addresses, or phone numbers. It does store Level 1 data tied to a flagged order: the Shopify Customer identifier, the coarsened /24 network prefix, and the customer order-history summary described in Section 1. Because this data can be associated with an order, and through your Shopify records with an identifiable customer, we treat the order-linked review artifacts and decision-log rows as personal data rather than as anonymous data. FraudPilot honors Shopify's customer privacy webhooks for this data:

  • A customers/data_request surfaces the review artifacts and decision-log rows we hold for the requested customer's orders.
  • A customers/redact request deletes the order-linked review artifacts, decision-log rows, and dispute outcomes for the specified orders.
  • A shop/redact request deletes all data for your shop.

These requests delete the data FraudPilot holds. They do not remove a fraudpilot-review tag from an order in your Shopify store; as the controller of your order records, you can remove the tag in the Shopify Admin if a customer's request requires it.

If a customer contacts you with a data request, you may contact us and we will assist. We respond to all data requests within 30 days.

8. International Data Transfers

Your data is processed and stored on servers located in the United States. By using the App, you consent to the transfer of your data to the United States for processing.

FraudPilot has no storefront component: it adds nothing to your storefront and collects nothing from your store's visitors. It reads two public pages of your storefront, described in Section 1, the same way any visitor could.
